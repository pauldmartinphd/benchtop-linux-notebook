From the working-session record, including [Bitwarden GNOME Issues](chatgpt-conversation://6adfeef1-66d0-83e8-bb3f-34341426f18f). Troubleshooting details added on October 28. Times are US Eastern.

## The installed laptop

- The desktop stuttered, with services timing out and crashing. The installed `/etc/fstab` had a plain `discard` on every Btrfs entry, which is synchronous discard. Changed by hand to `discard=async`.
- Snapper and transactional-update experienced prolonged stalls. The captured kernel stack was `bio_await → submit_bio_wait → blkdev_issue_discard → btrfs_issue_discard → btrfs_discard_extent → btrfs_finish_extent_commit → btrfs_commit_transaction`: the Btrfs transaction thread was waiting for synchronous discard during a transaction commit. The kernel also logged discard warnings with `errno=-512`; the warnings alone do not establish why the discard requests were slow.
- tuned-ppd failed to start: SELinux denied it writing to `/var/log/tuned/tuned-ppd.log`; the log directory `/var/log/tuned` was labelled `var_log_t` instead of `tuned_log_t`. Fixed with `restorecon -RF /var/log/tuned`; GNOME's power-mode menu works through tuned-ppd, which D-Bus starts on demand.
- `restorecon -RnvF` over `/var`, `/etc`, `/root`, `/srv`, `/opt` and `/usr/local` listed 1,090 files: mostly labels that differ only in the SELinux user, from files the live USB session created and the installer copied, and some files with no label at all (`unlabeled_t`). 27 had the wrong type, and 30 in `/usr`, all from the optional sets (Cockpit, Erlang, swtpm), labelled with the USB's policy rather than the new system's.
- Relabeled the writable filesystems and relabeled `/usr` in transactional snapshot 18. These corrected a separate SELinux labeling defect; the TuneD failure provides a concrete service-level consequence.
- Rebooted after changing `/etc/fstab` to `discard=async` and performing the SELinux repairs. I reported that everything was snappy again and GNOME Software worked properly. The blocked kernel transaction thread directly establishes synchronous discard in the commit path, and the recovery strongly supports it as a cause of the prolonged stalls. Because SELinux repairs occurred in the same session, the improvement cannot be attributed exclusively to discard.
- The journal is still not readable by wheel without sudo, as noted in Drivers and Firmware.

## Installer implications and remaining verification

- The installer-generated bare `discard` must be replaced with `discard=async` in `/etc/fstab`. Verify that the installer also uses asynchronous discard for its initial Btrfs mount and subsequent subvolume mounts; changing the generated fstab alone does not establish how the filesystem was mounted during installation.
- The same blocking mechanism is a strong explanation for the installer appearing to hang, but the captured stack came from the installed system. No kernel stack from the original installation establishes that cause directly.
- The installer must assign correct SELinux contexts before finalizing the immutable snapshot. The observed labeling defects establish the need for remediation; these manual repairs do not demonstrate that a new installation receives correct labels. See the October 27 entry for the subsequent installer changes and first-boot relabel approach.
- The reported reboot and restored responsiveness are confirmed observations. The available conversation does not contain results confirming that snapshot 18 became the active root, that the effective mount options after reboot were `discard=async`, that no new discard warnings appeared, or that a final relabel dry run was clean. These checks, along with a fresh installation and sustained snapshot/Flatpak/GNOME Software regression checks, remain unverified here.
